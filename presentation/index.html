Technique #1: XML Tags

Wrapping each content type in its own tag reduces misinterpretation. This is Anthropic's #1 reliability hack.

❌ Blob Prompt

Here's some text, can you summarize it
for a blog but make it funny and also check
for grammar and maybe turn it into bullet
points, here's the text: ...

✅ Structured Prompt

<instructions>
Summarize for a blog. Tone: witty.
Format: bullets. Check grammar.
</instructions>
<input>...text...</input>

Rule Use consistent, descriptive tag names. Nest hierarchies when needed: <documents> → <document index="1">

Technique #2: Few-Shot Examples

Examples are the most reliable way to steer output format, tone, and structure.

<examples>
  <example>
    <input>Refund request within 14 days</input>
    <output>{"decision": "approve", "reason": "within policy"}</output>
  </example>
  <example>
    <input>Refund after 90 days, no receipt</input>
    <output>{"decision": "escalate", "reason": "outside window"}</output>
  </example>
</examples>

Tip Make examples relevant (mirror real use cases), diverse (cover edge cases), and structured (wrap in <example> tags).

For large documents (20k+ tokens), put data at the TOP of your prompt — above instructions.

Anthropic measured up to 30% quality improvement when queries come at the end. 

<documents>...long docs...</documents>
<instructions>Summarize the key risks.</instructions>

Technique #3: Let Claude Think

For complex tasks, explicitly request reasoning before the answer.

Analyze this bug report. First, think through
possible root causes in <thinking> tags.
Then provide your final diagnosis in
<answer> tags.

Advanced On newer Claude models, enable thinking: {type: "adaptive"} for agentic behavior, multi-step tool use, and complex coding.

Self-check prompt: "Before you finish, verify your answer against [test criteria]." — catches errors reliably, especially for coding and math.

The 5-Pillar Framework

I operationalized Anthropic's teachings into a repeatable checklist. Here's how I evaluate every prompt:

1ClarityCould a literal-minded assistant misunderstand this?
2ContextWould the output change if Claude knew WHY?
3StructureCan Claude instantly parse instructions vs context?
4AdvancedWould drafting then critiquing improve quality?
5IterationSingle-shot or collaborative conversation?

The Canonical XML Template

This is the structured prompt format the skill generates. You can use it by hand today.

<role>[Persona — domain expert]</role>

<context>
  <background>[Situation]</background>
  <audience>[Who reads this]</audience>
  <source_material>[Optional data/docs]</source_material>
</context>

<objective>[Single, ultimate goal]</objective>

<instructions>
Break this into gated steps. Do not proceed until I approve:
Step 1: [Discovery]
Step 2: [Core deliverable]
Step 3: [Refinement — critique own work]
Step 4: [Final polish]
</instructions>

<format>[Exact output structure]</format>
<constraints>[Tone, length, exclusions, limitations]</constraints>
<review_criteria>[3+ checklist items]</review_criteria>
<first_action>[Specific first step]</first_action>

Example: Brand Messaging Guide

<role>
Senior Product Manager & Brand Strategist
</role>

<context>
  <background>
  Launching eco-friendly water bottle made
  from 100% recycled ocean plastics.
  </background>
  <audience>
  Environmentally conscious millennials
  (25-40), premium sustainable buyers.
  </audience>
</context>

<objective>
Create a brand messaging guide for the
web design team.
</objective>
<instructions>
Step 1: Propose 3 brand voice options.
Step 2: Write core messaging (tagline,
  mission, value props).
Step 3: Write homepage & FAQ copy.
Do not move to next step until approved.
</instructions>

<constraints>
- Tone: confident, no corporate jargon
- Length: homepage under 300 words
- Exclusions: no competitor names,
  no exclamation points
</constraints>

<review_criteria>
1. Does it align with chosen voice?
2. Is copy under 300 words?
3. Are claims backed by source material?
</review_criteria>

Example: AI Security Threat Analysis

Shows advanced patterns: named tensions, phased research, academic rigor.

<role>Lead AI Security Researcher & Strategic Business Analyst</role>

<context>
  <background>AI attack timelines compressed: 30 min → seconds.</background>
  <audience_paradox>
  Consumers = low friction, low spend.
  Enterprise = high ACV, high sales friction.
  Need the optimal "audience mode."
  </audience_paradox>
</context>

<instructions>
Phase 1: Threat landscape lit review. Rank top 3 AI threat vectors.
Phase 2: Audience segmentation & paradox resolution.
Phase 3: Impact modeling & monetization architecture.
Do not move to next phase until approved.
</instructions>

<constraints>
- Rigor: academic standard, no marketing fluff
- Objectivity: if unviable, state it plainly
</constraints>

3 Rules to Remember

1. Structure beats vibes

XML tags, not blob prompts. Separate instructions from context from input.

2. Examples are your superpower

2-3 diverse, relevant examples > clever wording every time.

3. Gate the work

Don't let Claude burn hours on a wrong direction. Break into steps. Approve each one.
